Install Offer Filter

Offer Filter is a free Android app for Dashers: set your minimums, and it declines Dasher offers below them. Setting it up takes about ten minutes, once, while you’re parked. The screens you’re most likely to see are below, in order.

↓Download Offer Filter

Android 15 or later. A signed APK from Offer Filter’s update server, dash-offer-filter-build.onrender.com, the same place the app gets its own updates.

Before you install

  • It’s a beta. It can misread an offer, decline one you wanted, or, if you turn on Auto-accept, accept one you didn’t want. Check your Dasher history.
  • Declines can lower your acceptance rate. Autopilot’s acceptance-rate goal (new in 0.5.0) is a best effort, not a promise: Autopilot can only stop declining, and your rate rises only when you accept offers. Nothing here guarantees a rate or any earnings.
  • It’s independent. Offer Filter isn’t made, endorsed or supported by DoorDash. DoorDash’s terms ban automated tools that read or collect data from its apps, and Offer Filter is one, so DoorDash could limit or deactivate your account.
  • It needs two Android permissions. Accessibility, to read Dasher’s screen and tap Decline, and notification access, to see Dasher’s offer alerts. Peek, on by default, briefly opens Dasher when an offer arrives while another app is in front; you can turn it off. During a dash it keeps your screen from timing out (new in 0.5.0), which uses more battery; it never wakes or unlocks your phone.
  • Set it up while parked. Don’t handle your phone while driving.

The app shows its terms and asks you to accept them before it does anything. Privacy explains what it reads and keeps.

Download and install

  1. Download the file

    Tap Download Offer Filter above. Chrome may say File might be harmful (older versions: This type of file can harm your device). Chrome says this about every app that doesn’t come from the Play Store. Tap Download anyway (or OK). Samsung Internet asks a similar question.

  2. Open it

    When the download finishes, tap Open in the message at the bottom of the screen. Missed it? Open Chrome’s ⋮ menu, then Downloads, or your Files app, and tap OfferFilter.apk.

    On a Samsung Galaxy, Auto Blocker may stop the install right here: see step 5.

  3. Let your browser install apps, once

    The first time, Android says that for your security your phone isn’t allowed to install unknown apps from this source. Tap Settings, turn on Allow from this source, go back, and tap Install.

    This applies only to the browser you used. You can turn it off again afterwards: Settings → Apps → Special app access → Install unknown apps (Samsung: Settings → Apps → ⋮ → Special access → Install unknown apps).

  4. Play Protect

    Google Play Protect may stop to check Offer Filter, because it’s new and doesn’t come from the Play Store. If you see Scan app, tap it: the scan takes a few seconds, and then you can install.

    If Play Protect says the app is harmful, or blocks it, stop there and don’t install it. Please tell us through Feedback so we can look into it. Never turn Play Protect off for Offer Filter.

  5. Samsung Galaxy: Auto Blocker

    If a Galaxy phone says Auto Blocker blocked the install, that’s Samsung’s Auto Blocker, at Settings → Security and privacy → Auto Blocker. While it’s on, it blocks apps from anywhere except Galaxy Store and Google Play, and that includes Offer Filter’s own updates later.

    Turning it off lowers that protection for every app, not only this one. If you’re not comfortable with that, it’s fine to stop here. If you turn it off, open OfferFilter.apk again (step 2) to install, and Offer Filter can then install and update itself. You can turn Auto Blocker back on at any time, but it then blocks Offer Filter’s updates too: Offer Filter’s Settings → Updates says so (step 18), and that update isn’t tried again by itself. Turn Auto Blocker off, then tap Updates there to try again.

First run

  1. Read the notice

    Open Offer Filter. Its first screen explains what it reads, the acceptance-rate risk and the terms. Tap I understand and accept only if you agree. Not now closes the app without it doing anything.

    New in 0.6.34 Please turn on Share anonymous diagnostics at the bottom of the notice, so problems on your phone get fixed. It’s one switch for the summary after each dash and masked diagnostics with feedback you send. It stays off unless you turn it on, and you can turn it off in Settings.

  2. Turn on screen reading

    New in 0.6.26 Until Offer Filter can filter, its setup fills the screen: one step at a time, each with one button, and each says what to do before Android’s screen opens. The first, Turn on screen reading, is Android’s Accessibility setting. Tap Open Accessibility, then Installed apps (Downloaded apps on phones other than Samsung), then Offer Filter, and turn it on. Android warns that the app can see and control your screen; that’s how it reads offers and taps Decline in Dasher.

    Once it’s on, Offer Filter comes back by itself where Android lets it; otherwise go back to it. Stuck? Send feedback, at the foot of every step, opens the feedback form.

    On Android 12 and later, about a day later Android may remind you once, with a notification, that Offer Filter can view and control your screen. That’s expected; turning it off there stops Offer Filter. If screen reading or background offers stop working later, setup comes back with just that step: Restart screen reading or Reconnect background offers.

  3. Android 13 and later: “App was denied access” or “Restricted setting”

    Because the app came from your browser, Android blocks screen reading’s switch the first time, every time the app is installed. Setup says so before you go, and it’s expected: Android says App was denied access (“Access to this permission can put your personal and financial info at risk”) or Restricted setting (“For your security, this setting is currently unavailable”). Close the message (Close or OK) and go back to Offer Filter.

    1. Setup’s step is now Allow restricted settings. Tap Open App info, then ⋮ at the top right, then Allow restricted settings, confirm with your PIN or fingerprint, and go back.
    2. Now turn on screen reading opens Accessibility again: Settings → Accessibility → Installed apps → Offer Filter (Downloaded apps on some phones). Turn it on.

    Allow restricted settings appears in the ⋮ menu only after the switch was tapped once, which is why Android’s message is expected; until then Offer Filter is listed in Accessibility as Controlled by Restricted Setting. If App info has no such item, go back and tap Try the switch again under setup’s button, then open App info again. Allowing it once covers notification access too. To reach App info yourself, touch and hold the Offer Filter icon and tap App info, or go to Settings → Apps → Offer Filter.

  4. Turn on background offers

    Offer Filter also reads Dasher’s offer notifications, so it catches offers while Dasher is in the background. Tap Open notification access and turn on Offer Filter background offers. It reads only Dasher’s notifications and ignores every other app. If Android blocks this switch too, setup shows Allow restricted settings again, with the same App info steps.

  5. Allow alerts, and the map if you like

    Allow alerts: Android asks Allow Offer Filter to send you notifications? Tap Allow. Offer Filter uses them for offers it lets through or can’t read, to say when it pauses, and for update notices.

    Map where offers pay best: Allow location lets Offer Filter pin each offer where it came in, so its map shows where pay is best. It asks only for your approximate location, and it’s optional: filtering doesn’t need it, and Not now skips it. The map appears on the home screen once location is allowed, and Settings has its switch.

  6. Set your minimums

    New in 0.6.26 Setup’s last step, Start with typical minimums, offers $2.00 per stop ($4.00 for a single order), $1.00 per mile and $15 per hour of trip time, typical of Cincinnati offers seen in October 2026. Start auto-decline saves them and turns auto-decline on. Set my own leaves the minimums to you: drag a knob on the home screen, then tap the mascot to turn auto-decline on. Auto-accept stays off unless you turn it on separately in Settings and confirm.

    There are three minimums, one knob each on the home screen’s constellation: pay per stop, counting every stop Dasher shows, the pickup included, pay per mile and pay per hour of Dasher’s own time estimate. An offer has to meet each one you set. You can also set max stops, the most stops you’ll take. Offers below your minimums are declined; the rest are left to you.

    New in 0.6.1 Your accepts teach. With Learn on (the round button on the constellation, on by default), an offer you accept yourself that beats your best pay, pay per mile or pay per hour raises a learned minimum. Offers must meet it as well as yours, and your knobs stay where you set them. A note on the home screen, Learned minimums rose, shows what it asks, with Adopt to make it yours. Long-press Learn to see what it learned, with Adopt, Undo and Reset.

    Updating from an older version? Your numbers stay, with per stop and your old percentage built in, rounded up so nothing gets looser. Auto-accept is turned off once if your rules changed meaning, and 0.6.2 turns Autopilot on once, keeping a goal you chose before. A one-time note, Your rules are simpler now, lists what changed.

Before your first dash

  1. Check Autopilot’s goal New in 0.6.2

    Autopilot is on from the start, with its suggested goal, pay first. Its Auto chip sits at the start of the status line, at the top of the home screen. The first time you turn auto-decline on with the mascot, it asks once, What matters more?

    • Pay first: push for better pay, no acceptance-rate goal. Suggested and preselected.
    • Protect my acceptance rate: 70% or more (a top tier).
    • Protect my acceptance rate: 50% or more (a tier).

    One tap applies, and Not now keeps the suggested goal. Long-press the Auto chip to change the goal any time. Tap it for Autopilot’s details, where Turn off judges offers at exactly your minimums; it stays off until you turn it back on.

    Autopilot moves one bar: how much of your minimums an offer must pay, from 10% to 150%. It goes by how often offers come, what your recent offers paid for Dasher’s time, and the acceptance rate Dasher shows on its decline question, keeping enough offers passing to reach your goal and stay a little above it. Above 100% it declines some offers that meet your minimums. Below 100%, offers it lets through under your minimums are yours to decide: never declined and never auto-accepted. DoorDash computes acceptance rate over your recent offers (it reports the last 100), and its rewards tiers use acceptance-rate minimums; check the Dasher app for your current requirements.

    New in 0.6.29 Your accepts set Autopilot’s minimums. With Autopilot on, each offer you accept yourself sets Autopilot’s working pay per stop, per mile and per hour to that offer’s own values, each up or down; the newest accept replaces the last. Autopilot never changes your saved minimums: they change only when you set them or tap Adopt, and Learn separately raises learned minimums when you accept an offer that beats your best. After an accept, Autopilot holds its bar for your next 24 offers, across dashes; tap the Auto chip to see its working minimums and the countdown.

    New in 0.6.37 Your declines raise the bar. When you decline an offer yourself, Autopilot raises its bar to just above that offer, so offers no better are declined, and holds it there for your next 24 offers, even during an accept’s hold. It never lowers the bar, and your next accept replaces it. Offers you let time out, and offers you decline while paused, don’t count.

    New in 0.6.31 Total pay counts. In Area mode, a quarter of each Autopilot score is an offer’s total pay against your latest accept’s, so a bigger offer a little under that accept’s rates can pass, and a smaller one at its rates can be declined. It’s total against total, never pay per stop times stops; the chart’s pay line shows that accept’s total at the bar.

    Its goal is a best effort, not a promise: Autopilot can only stop declining, and your acceptance rate rises only when you accept offers. It’s new: watch how it does on your first dashes.

  2. Dash in Earn per Offer

    Offer Filter works with Earn per Offer. Earn by Time isn’t supported in this beta, so pause Offer Filter if you dash by time.

  3. Pick a driving layout

    Set it up before you drive:

    • Dasher full screen, with Offer Filter’s tab on the edge. New in 0.6.33 Tap the tab to come back to Offer Filter. New in 0.6.34 The same tab, with Dasher’s icon, sits at the same edge of Offer Filter’s home screen: tap it to go back to Dasher. Moving one moves both.
    • Google Maps or Waze full screen, with Peek on. For a new offer, Offer Filter briefly opens Dasher, then brings you back to your map. New in 0.5.0 If an offer pulled you out of your map and ended without you accepting it, Back to map under the tab takes you back with one tap.

While you dash

  1. Pause any time

    Tap the mascot on Offer Filter’s home screen to pause or resume; your rules stay. New in 0.6.33 Over Dasher, touch and hold the tab to do the same. If anything seems off, pause it and use Dasher directly. To stop completely, turn off Offer Filter’s Accessibility and notification access, or uninstall it.

    New in 0.6.35 Paused, nothing is declined, but Offer Filter still reads each offer on Dasher’s screen and shows its score beside the tab, outlined in red when it would decline. Offers you take while paused don’t move Autopilot or learning. To stop it reading Dasher at all, turn off Offer Filter in Android’s Accessibility settings.

  2. Let Peek check background offers

    With Peek on (Settings → Peek at background offers, on by default), an offer that arrives while another app is in front briefly opens Dasher, so Offer Filter can read it and apply your rules. After a decline it takes you back to the app you were in. An offer that passes stays in Dasher for you, unless you’re navigating: then you go back to your map with a card showing the offer. Peek needs an unlocked phone and a quiet moment: it waits until your finger is off the screen, and skips during a call or while you type.

    New in 0.5.0 Peek waits for Dasher’s offer while DoorDash’s notification is still up. If Dasher opens without it, Peek taps Dasher’s own notification once; if the offer still doesn’t show, a card says so. New in 0.5.2 The same tap comes once when Dasher is already on screen, in front or beside Offer Filter, still showing Finding offers 5 seconds after an offer’s notification. If an offer dings while your phone is locked, unlock within 40 seconds and Peek checks it for you, unless you open Dasher yourself first. If Peek pauses itself after repeated failures, the home screen says Peek paused with Resume; it never turns its own switch off.

    New in 0.5.0 During a dash, with auto-decline on, Offer Filter keeps your screen from timing out, whatever app is in front, so Peek can work. It never wakes or unlocks the phone, and the power button still turns the screen off. It lets the screen sleep with battery saver on, at 20% battery or less while not charging, after 15 minutes with no sign of your dash, and once you end your dash in Dasher. This uses more battery. Peek pauses while your phone is locked.

  3. Tell us what happened

    Use Send anonymous feedback in Offer Filter’s Settings (new in 0.5.0), or the feedback form on this site. No account needed. Your phone model and Android version help; leave out customer names, addresses and payment details. We can’t reply to anonymous messages.

Updates

  1. Let Offer Filter install its own updates

    New in 0.6.26 Nothing asks for this until an update is ready. Then, when you’re not dashing, the home screen shows Update ready · Allow updates: tap it and turn on Allow from this source. This is separate from your browser’s permission in step 3. Updates are signed and checked before they install. Keep the app installed; there’s no need to uninstall to update.

    With updates allowed, the line says Update ready · Install now, and Settings → Updates shows where updates stand. Automatic installs wait until a dash ends, or until nothing of a dash has been seen for 8 hours with the screen off. If Auto Blocker stops an update, Settings → Updates says so: “Android blocked the update (on Samsung, check Auto Blocker). Tap Updates to try again.” Turn Auto Blocker off first; the blocked update waits for that tap.

    Already on 0.4.72? It installs an update by itself only after it has seen your dash end, and it has no Install now line or 8-hour limit (those start with 0.5.0). If its Settings → Updates says Update ready: installs after your dash while you’re not dashing (for example because you turned Offer Filter’s Accessibility off to get through 0.4.72’s frozen Dasher), tap Updates once with Dasher not on screen, or download 0.5.0 above and install it over the old app. Your rules and history stay. If you turned Accessibility off, turn it back on afterwards.
