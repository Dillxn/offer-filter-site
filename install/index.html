Install Offer Filter

Offer Filter is a free Android app for Dashers: set your minimums, and it declines Dasher offers below them. Setting it up takes about ten minutes, once, while you’re parked. The screens you’re most likely to see are below, in order.

↓Download Offer Filter

Android 8 or later. A signed APK from Offer Filter’s update server, dash-offer-filter-build.onrender.com, the same place the app gets its own updates.

Before you install

  • It’s a beta. It can misread an offer, decline one you wanted, or, if you turn on Auto-accept, accept one you didn’t want. Check your Dasher history.
  • Declines can lower your acceptance rate. Autopilot’s acceptance-rate goal (new in 0.5.0) is a best effort, not a promise: Autopilot can only stop declining, and your rate rises only when you accept offers. Nothing here guarantees a rate or any earnings.
  • It’s independent. Offer Filter isn’t made, endorsed or supported by DoorDash. Using it may conflict with DoorDash’s terms, and DoorDash could limit or deactivate your account.
  • It needs two Android permissions. Accessibility, to read Dasher’s screen and tap Decline, and notification access, to see Dasher’s offer alerts. Peek, on by default, briefly opens Dasher when an offer arrives while another app is in front; you can turn it off. During a dash it keeps your screen from timing out (new in 0.5.0), which uses more battery; it never wakes or unlocks your phone.
  • Set it up while parked. Don’t handle your phone while driving.

The app shows its terms and asks you to accept them before it does anything. Privacy explains what it reads and keeps.

Download and install

  1. Download the file

    Tap Download Offer Filter above. Chrome may say File might be harmful (older versions: This type of file can harm your device). Chrome says this about every app that doesn’t come from the Play Store. Tap Download anyway (or OK). Samsung Internet asks a similar question.

  2. Open it

    When the download finishes, tap Open in the message at the bottom of the screen. Missed it? Open Chrome’s ⋮ menu, then Downloads, or your Files app, and tap OfferFilter.apk.

    On a Samsung Galaxy, Auto Blocker may stop the install right here: see step 5.

  3. Let your browser install apps, once

    The first time, Android says that for your security your phone isn’t allowed to install unknown apps from this source. Tap Settings, turn on Allow from this source, go back, and tap Install.

    This applies only to the browser you used. You can turn it off again afterwards: Settings → Apps → Special app access → Install unknown apps (Samsung: Settings → Apps → ⋮ → Special access → Install unknown apps).

  4. Play Protect

    Google Play Protect may stop to check Offer Filter, because it’s new and doesn’t come from the Play Store. If you see Scan app, tap it: the scan takes a few seconds, and then you can install.

    If Play Protect says the app is harmful, or blocks it, stop there and don’t install it. Please tell us through Feedback so we can look into it. Never turn Play Protect off for Offer Filter.

  5. Samsung Galaxy: Auto Blocker

    If a Galaxy phone says Auto Blocker blocked the install, that’s Samsung’s Auto Blocker, at Settings → Security and privacy → Auto Blocker. While it’s on, it blocks apps from anywhere except Galaxy Store and Google Play, and that includes Offer Filter’s own updates later.

    Turning it off lowers that protection for every app, not only this one. If you’re not comfortable with that, it’s fine to stop here. If you turn it off, open OfferFilter.apk again (step 2) to install, and Offer Filter can then install and update itself. You can turn Auto Blocker back on at any time, but it then blocks Offer Filter’s updates too: Offer Filter’s Settings → Updates says so (step 10), and that update isn’t tried again by itself. Turn Auto Blocker off, then tap Updates there to try again.

First run

  1. Read the notice

    Open Offer Filter. Its first screen explains what it reads, the acceptance-rate risk and the terms. Tap I understand and accept only if you agree. Not now closes the app without it doing anything.

  2. Turn on what it needs

    Offer Filter’s home screen lists the Android steps still to do, each with a Fix button that opens the right Android screen. Do them in order.

    New in 0.5.0 The steps are numbered, one line each and only while needed. With three or more to do, the first shows and N more to set up · Show lists the rest:

    1. Allow restricted settings (Android 13 and later, when the app came from your browser): see step 8.
    2. Turn on Offer Filter in Accessibility: in Accessibility, open Downloaded apps (Installed apps on Samsung), choose Offer Filter and turn it on. Android warns that the app can see and control your screen; that’s how it reads offers and taps Decline in Dasher. On Android 12 and later, about a day later Android may remind you once, with a notification, that Offer Filter can view and control your screen. That’s expected; turning it off there stops Offer Filter.
    3. Allow notification access: choose Offer Filter background offers and allow it. It reads only Dasher’s notifications and ignores every other app.
    4. Allow alerts: Offer Filter’s own notifications (step 9).
    5. Allow updates: shows once the rest is done, or right away when an update is waiting (step 10).

    If something you turned on stops working, its line says Turn Offer Filter off and on in Accessibility or Reconnect notification access instead.

  3. Android 13 and later: “Restricted setting” or “App was denied access”

    Because the app came from your browser, Android 13 and later keep Accessibility and notification access locked for it at first, every time it’s installed. Offer Filter’s first step, Allow restricted settings, leads you through this before you hit the lock: tap Fix, then Try the switch. You’ll see one of these:

    • The switch is greyed out, or Offer Filter is listed as Controlled by Restricted Setting.
    • Restricted setting (“For your security, this setting is currently unavailable”).
    • On newer Samsung Galaxy phones: App was denied access (“Access to this permission can put your personal and financial info at risk”).

    Close the message (OK or Close), then:

    1. Tap Open App info in Offer Filter’s step (or touch and hold the Offer Filter icon and tap App info, or go to Settings → Apps → Offer Filter).
    2. Tap ⋮ at the top right, then Allow restricted settings, and confirm with your PIN or fingerprint.
    3. Go back to Offer Filter. The setting you were turning on opens by itself if you come back within ten minutes; turn the switch on there (for Accessibility, Settings → Accessibility → Installed apps → Offer Filter, or Downloaded apps on some phones). Otherwise tap Fix again, and if you already allowed it, choose Try the switch.

    Allow restricted settings only appears after you’ve tried the switch once, so the message above is expected: it’s what unlocks the menu item. Not in the ⋮ menu yet? Try the switch once more, then open App info again. Allowing it once covers notification access too. The step goes away once it worked. Android 8 to 12 skip this step.

  4. Allow alerts

    Tap Fix beside Allow alerts. On Android 13 and later, Android asks Allow Offer Filter to send you notifications? Tap Allow. Offer Filter uses them for offers it lets through or can’t read, and for update notices. On Android 8 to 12 they’re on unless you turned them off.

  5. Let Offer Filter install its own updates

    On Offer Filter’s home screen, tap Fix beside Allow updates and turn on Allow from this source. This is separate from your browser’s permission in step 3. Updates are signed and checked before they install. Keep the app installed; there’s no need to uninstall to update.

    When an update is ready and you’re not dashing, the home screen shows Update ready · Install now, and Settings → Updates shows where updates stand. Automatic installs wait until a dash ends, or until nothing of a dash has been seen for 8 hours with the screen off. If Auto Blocker stops an update, Settings → Updates says so: “Android blocked the update (on Samsung, check Auto Blocker). Tap Updates to try again.” Turn Auto Blocker off first; the blocked update waits for that tap.

    Already on 0.4.72? It installs an update by itself only after it has seen your dash end, and it has no Install now line or 8-hour limit (those start with 0.5.0). If its Settings → Updates says Update ready: installs after your dash while you’re not dashing (for example because you turned Offer Filter’s Accessibility off to get through 0.4.72’s frozen Dasher), tap Updates once with Dasher not on screen, or download 0.5.0 above and install it over the old app. Your rules and history stay. If you turned Accessibility off, turn it back on afterwards.

Before your first dash

  1. Set your minimums

    New in 0.5.0 There are three, set by dragging the knobs on the home screen’s constellation: minimum pay, pay per mile and pay per hour of Dasher’s own time estimate. An offer has to meet each one you set. You can also set max stops, the most stops you’ll take. There’s no per-item or per-stop minimum: shopping time is already in Dasher’s minutes, and max stops limits stacked orders. Offers below your minimums are declined; the rest are left to you.

    With no rule yet, the home screen says Tap to start with typical minimums. Its Use these sets $4.00 per offer, $1.00 per mile and $15 per hour, typical of Cincinnati offers seen in October 2026; drag any knob to change them. Auto-decline starts only when you tap the mascot. Auto-accept stays off unless you turn it on separately in Settings and confirm.

    Updating from an older version? Your numbers stay, with per stop and your old percentage built in, rounded up so nothing gets looser. Autopilot starts off, and Auto-accept is turned off once if your rules changed meaning. A one-time note, Your rules are simpler now, lists what changed.

  2. Turn on Autopilot New in 0.5.0

    Tap the round Auto button on the constellation. Autopilot asks What matters more?

    • Keep a top tier: acceptance rate 70% or more. Suggested and preselected.
    • Keep a tier: acceptance rate 50% or more.
    • Pay first: no acceptance-rate goal.

    One tap applies. Long-press the Auto button to change the goal any time; a tap turns Autopilot off, and offers are then judged at exactly your minimums. The question also comes by itself after Use these, or the first time you turn auto-decline on with a minimum set.

    Autopilot moves one bar: how much of your minimums an offer must pay, from 50% to 150%. It goes by how often offers come, what your recent offers paid for Dasher’s time, and the acceptance rate Dasher shows on its decline question, keeping enough offers passing to reach your goal and stay a little above it. Above 100% it declines some offers that meet your minimums. Below 100%, offers it lets through under your minimums are yours to decide: never declined and never auto-accepted. DoorDash computes acceptance rate over your recent offers (it reports the last 100), and its rewards tiers use acceptance-rate minimums; check the Dasher app for your current requirements.

    Its goal is a best effort, not a promise: Autopilot can only stop declining, and your acceptance rate rises only when you accept offers. It’s new: watch how it does on your first dashes.

  3. Dash in Earn per Offer

    Offer Filter works with Earn per Offer. Earn by Time isn’t supported in this beta, so pause Offer Filter if you dash by time.

  4. Pick a driving layout

    Set it up before you drive:

    • Dasher full screen, with Offer Filter’s tab on the edge.
    • Google Maps or Waze full screen, with Peek on. For a new offer, Offer Filter briefly opens Dasher, then brings you back to your map. New in 0.5.0 If an offer pulled you out of your map and ended without you accepting it, Back to map under the tab takes you back with one tap.
    • Maps and Dasher in split screen, with the tab on Dasher’s side. New in 0.5.0

    Offer Filter’s own split screen is for planning while parked, not for driving: beside a map, background offers need a tap there. New in 0.5.0 Split beside Dasher, drag the divider toward Offer Filter to give Dasher’s map about two thirds of the screen; Offer Filter becomes a strip with the mascot, the latest verdict and Autopilot’s chip.

While you dash

  1. Pause any time

    Tap the mascot on Offer Filter’s home screen to pause or resume; your rules stay. If anything seems off, pause it and use Dasher directly. To stop completely, turn off Offer Filter’s Accessibility and notification access, or uninstall it.

    New in 0.5.0 Paused means Offer Filter isn’t reading Dasher at all: nothing is declined, and offers on Dasher’s screen aren’t judged or added to your history until you resume. Once Dasher’s screen changes, the tab shrinks to a slim bar that takes no touches, so resume from Offer Filter’s home screen.

  2. Let Peek check background offers

    With Peek on (Settings → Peek at background offers, on by default), an offer that arrives while another app is in front briefly opens Dasher, so Offer Filter can read it and apply your rules. After a decline it takes you back to the app you were in. An offer that passes stays in Dasher for you, unless you’re navigating: then you go back to your map with a card showing the offer. Peek needs an unlocked phone and a quiet moment: it waits until your finger is off the screen, and skips during a call or while you type.

    New in 0.5.0 Peek waits for Dasher’s offer while DoorDash’s notification is still up. If Dasher opens without it, Peek taps Dasher’s own notification once; if the offer still doesn’t show, a card says so. If an offer dings while your phone is locked, unlock within 40 seconds and Peek checks it for you, unless you open Dasher yourself first. If Peek pauses itself after repeated failures, the home screen says Peek paused with Resume; it never turns its own switch off.

    New in 0.5.0 During a dash, with auto-decline on, Offer Filter keeps your screen from timing out, whatever app is in front, so Peek can work. It never wakes or unlocks the phone, and the power button still turns the screen off. It lets the screen sleep with battery saver on, at 20% battery or less while not charging, after 15 minutes with no sign of your dash, and once you end your dash in Dasher. This uses more battery. Peek pauses while your phone is locked.

  3. Tell us what happened

    Use Send anonymous feedback in Offer Filter’s Settings (new in 0.5.0), or the feedback form on this site. No account needed. Your phone model and Android version help; leave out customer names, addresses and payment details. We can’t reply to anonymous messages.

What’s in the 0.5.0 beta

  • Three minimums. Minimum pay, pay per mile and pay per hour, plus max stops. Score by area, per stop, per item and the learned minimums are gone. An update keeps your numbers, with per stop and your old percentage built in.
  • Autopilot. Optional. It asks what matters more (an acceptance rate of 70% or more, suggested; 50% or more; or pay first) and moves how much of your minimums an offer must pay, from 50% to 150%, using your real acceptance rate from Dasher’s decline question. Offers below your minimums stay yours.
  • Setup on the home screen. Numbered steps, from Allow restricted settings to Allow updates, each with a Fix button.
  • Dasher stays responsive. Lighter screen reading, and paused means Offer Filter isn’t reading Dasher at all.
  • Better Peek. It waits for Dasher’s offer, checks one that dinged while your phone was locked when you unlock in time, and your screen doesn’t time out mid-dash.
  • Driving layouts. The tab over Dasher’s half beside a map, Back to map, and a driving strip when Offer Filter has a third of a split screen.
  • Fewer notifications. No duplicate cards when Dasher’s own notification already has the offer.
  • Feedback without an account. Send it from the app or this site. Optional diagnostics: turn on Attach masked diagnostics for one message, or Share anonymous diagnostics after each dash in Settings. Both start off.

It’s a beta: it can misread an offer, and it makes no promise about earnings or your acceptance rate.
